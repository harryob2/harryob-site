Chapter I
Escape from Peril

The book. The Quest for Certainty: A Study of the Relation of Knowledge and Action grew out of the Gifford Lectures that John Dewey (1859–1952), the American pragmatist, gave at Edinburgh in 1929. Its target is a split that runs through Western thought: theory above practice, knowing above doing.

This chapter is the diagnosis. Dewey argues that the split is not a discovery of reason but a legacy of fear. Living among hazards, people sought safety either by appeasing the powers that rule their fate or by mastering nature through the arts. Because practical action is always uncertain, they came to imagine a higher realm of fixed, eternal Being that pure thought could grasp with complete certainty. Greek philosophy turned that old religious division into a theory of reality and of knowledge, and the theory still governs how we think about mind, truth and value.
